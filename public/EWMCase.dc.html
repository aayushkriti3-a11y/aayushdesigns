01 · INGREDIENTS

A powerful product that feels older than it is useful

EWM helps engineering teams plan, track and ship work, with deep traceability and governance. It's also around 25 years old, its front end carries growing technical debt, and customers compare it with Jira every day.

A rebuild was on the table. Before anyone redrew a screen, the team needed evidence of what to keep, what to fix, and where to start.

THE CORE FINDING Customers don't want a simpler engineering product. They want a simpler experience.
KEEP

Traceability, governance and configurability: the reasons customers chose EWM.

FIX

How it feels to use: speed, navigation and everyday tasks.

WORK WITHIN

IBM Carbon, EWM's existing vocabulary, and customers with very large, customised setups.

03 · METHOD

Starting with the most frequent dish: creating a work item

Creating a work item is one of the most frequent things anyone does in EWM, and today it pulls people away from what they were doing. I took it on first. Three decisions shaped it.

  1. 1Create from where you areCreate starts on the page you're working on and opens in a panel beside your list, so you never lose your place. Rejected: one global Create button in the header. It copies Jira without solving EWM's problem.
  2. 2Design for scale, not for the demoCustomers run 20+ work item types and hundreds of custom fields. Summary comes first, small fields sit three to a row, labels keep EWM's own words, and pickers open inline instead of in pop-ups. Rejected: a roomy, one-field-per-row form that looks great with five fields and breaks at fifty.
  3. 3Four clear ways to create, kept separateFrom scratch, from a template tied to the type, many items at once from a template, and import from CSV. Templates now sit right next to Type, which closes a real gap: today templates and types aren't connected at all.
